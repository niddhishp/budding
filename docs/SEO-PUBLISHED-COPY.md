### Who is behind the words?

Kahiye Editorial publishes practical guides for everyday parenting, with links to the sources behind each topic. The guides include AI-assisted drafting and are not presented as independently clinically reviewed. Use them to prepare a conversation or a question for your clinician, not to diagnose your child.

#### A byline you can understand.

“Kahiye Editorial” is the site’s publishing byline. It does not identify a licensed clinician or imply that a named expert has approved the text. Individual author and reviewer biographies will be added when their roles and qualifications are confirmed.

#### Sources you can follow.

Each guide links to the source material used for its topic, including public health and child-protection organisations. A source link does not mean that organisation endorses Kahiye. Advice and services may differ by country, and a clinician who knows your child can help you apply it.

The date on a guide reflects a content or presentation update. It is not a clinical approval date. We avoid presenting age ranges as deadlines or a short quiz as a diagnosis.

#### How AI is involved.

AI assists with drafting and editing, and generates personalised responses in Ask. Automated checks can catch some problems, but they do not replace human editorial judgment, safeguarding expertise or clinical review. We do not describe this material as entirely human-authored or clinically validated.

#### Review still to come.

Independent clinical and safeguarding review is pending. Named reviewers, their relevant qualifications and the scope of each completed review will be published when available. Until then, use these guides for general orientation and bring health or safety concerns to an appropriate professional.

#### If something looks wrong.

A public corrections inbox is being set up. We will publish the address here once it is ready to receive and respond to messages. Please do not send private health details through public comments or social media. For an urgent health or safety concern, contact an appropriate professional directly.

Only to give you parenting guidance tailored to your child, keep your history, run your subscription, and keep the service safe. We do not sell your data, provide it for targeted advertising by other companies, or share it with other parents.

### Before puberty: make questions welcome

Build a language for bodies, privacy and growing up, without a big serious talk.

Kahiye Editorial (/editorial) · Updated 2026-10-20
0–4 years / 5–7 years

#### What to know

For babies, this is a caregiver habit: explain care gently. As language grows, use accurate body-part names, including penis and vulva. Older children can hear that puberty means bodies gradually change towards adulthood. There is no need to describe every change at once.

#### Words you can borrow

“You can ask me about any part of your body. If I don’t know, we can find a good answer together.”

#### Try together

Choose an ordinary moment, such as getting ready for a bath. Ask what they wonder about growing up; answer the actual question in one or two sentences.

#### A small practice moment

Your child asks a body question in front of relatives. “That is a good question. Let’s find a quiet place to talk.” Follow through, without laughing or making the question feel shameful.

#### Care and support

Age bands organise conversations; they do not predict physical development. Ask a clinician about changes that seem unusually early or cause concern.

Breast changes, body hair and a calm introduction to periods.

Kahiye Editorial (/editorial) · Updated 2026-10-20
8–10 years

#### What to know

Breast development often begins between 8 and 13. One side may start first and feel tender. Hair, sweat and body shape can change too. These are possibilities, not a checklist to inspect. A period is blood and tissue leaving the uterus through the vagina; it is not an injury or something dirty.

#### Words you can borrow

“Your body has its own pace. You don’t have to compare it with anyone else’s. Would you like me to explain periods now, or find a book together?”

Offer comfortable underwear or a first bra if wanted. Put a pad and spare underwear in a small school pouch; explain where to ask for help.

#### A small practice moment

“Everyone will notice my body changing.” Acknowledge the worry. Offer choices about clothing and who they talk to; do not announce their changes to family.

#### Care and support

Ask a paediatrician about breast changes before 8, absent pubertal changes by 13, or any persistent pain or worry.

Kahiye Editorial (/editorial) · Updated 2026-10-20
11–13 years

#### What to know

“If your period starts at school, you can ask your trusted teacher for the pouch. You can call me too. There is nothing to be embarrassed about.”

#### A small practice moment

They want to skip school because of cramps. Listen to the pain rather than assuming avoidance. Offer comfort and ask a clinician about suitable treatment if pain disrupts normal activities.

#### Care and support

Seek medical advice for heavy bleeding, such as soaking products every 1–2 hours, or pain affecting daily life. Fainting or severe illness needs urgent care.

### A way out of pressure, every time

Support safer decisions about substances, controlling relationships and difficult social situations.

14–17 years

#### What to know

State clear expectations about avoiding alcohol, nicotine and drugs while keeping help available. Manipulation can sound like “prove you love me”, demands for passwords, or threats to share private information. A relationship does not create an obligation to comply.

#### Words you can borrow

“If you feel trapped, or you have already taken something, call me. Getting you safe comes first. We can talk about everything else afterwards.”

#### Try together

Agree a pickup plan, a backup adult and a code word. Discuss never riding with an impaired driver. Ask what makes refusal hardest in their own social circle.

#### A small practice moment

A friend is unresponsive after taking an unknown substance. Get emergency medical help immediately, stay with them if safe, and tell responders what is known. Do not leave them alone to sleep it off.

#### Care and support
