# Kahiye: earned coverage and Forbes outreach

Prepared 20 October 2026. Draft only. No messages have been sent, no backlink has been secured, and coverage is not guaranteed.

## The story worth pitching

“Personalisation without turning parenting into another shopping profile.”

Kahiye's useful tension is that it aims to help parents anticipate difficult conversations, while product referrals form part of its business model. The credible story is how the company handles that tension: optional and editable memory, separate family records, labelled referral links, no commission-based product ranking, and clear limits on AI guidance.

Do not pitch it as the first parenting app, an AI therapist, a clinically proven intervention or an app that has made parents better. Those claims are not substantiated. A technical evaluation with fictional scenarios is not evidence of benefits for real families.

## Personalised pitch — ready for a founder to review

**Subject:** A parenting app testing the limits of “personalisation”

Hi Christine,

Your piece on AI mom marketing raised a question we are working through at Kahiye: how can a product remember what helps a family without reducing a parent to a buying profile?

We are building a parenting handbook with help for the moment a parent is in: wording for a difficult conversation, guidance for the next stage, and a place for family plans. Parents can inspect, change or pause the memories used for future suggestions.

There is a commercial tension here. Our model includes subscriptions, partnerships and product referrals. We label referrals, and commissions do not determine product rankings. We would be happy to show the implementation and discuss where that approach still needs work.

If you revisit AI and the work of caregiving, could this be a useful case study? We can offer a founder walkthrough using fictional families, the memory controls, and our technical safety-testing process. Independent clinical review is still pending; we are not claiming clinical outcomes.

The public overview is at https://www.kahiye.app/about and the editorial limits are at https://www.kahiye.app/editorial.

## Before sending

1. Set up a monitored company mailbox and use a real founder signature. Complete the legal entity and public contact information on the site.
2. Secure an independent clinical/safeguarding reviewer for claims that touch health or safety, or keep the pitch strictly about product design and engineering. Explain the current review status without ambiguity.
3. Prepare a small press folder: a factual founder bio, three screenshots with fictional profiles, the product's current availability, a 90-second demo and the editorial policy. Never include children's private records or screenshots from a real account.
4. Publish a substantive methodology page before offering it as a reporting resource: how parent memory is controlled, what fictional-scenario tests cover, what they do not establish, referral ranking constraints, known failure modes and review ownership. The current editorial page is an introduction, not a substitute for that methodology.
5. Check every claim in the email against the deployed product. If clinical review, paid checkout or provider availability changes, update the public pages and pitch together.

## Broader link opportunities

- Parent education organisations and schools: offer a genuinely useful, reviewed communication handout. An optional source link to the relevant guide is more natural than a demand for a homepage link.
- Qualified therapists and paediatric educators: collaborate on a publicly attributed guide only after the review is complete. Disclose commercial relationships.
- Family and workplace publications: offer specific work on sharing caregiving responsibilities and communicating with children. Match each pitch to current published coverage.
- Relevant communities: answer the actual question and disclose the Kahiye relationship when linking. Avoid forum spam, automated comments, link exchanges and paid “authority” packages.

An editor decides whether to link and whether a link is followed. Referral traffic, relevant readership and accurate coverage matter more than a headline domain-authority score.
